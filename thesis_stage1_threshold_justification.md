# Justification of the Stage-1 SingleR thresholds

Supporting analysis for Table 3 (Stage 1). Generated by
`thesis_plots/stage1_threshold_sensitivity.py`; raw sweeps in
`thesis_plots/stage1_sensitivity/`.

The three thresholds are: the `score_tumor` floor for tumor **candidates**
(0.2), the `score_tumor` floor for the tumor **reference** set (0.4), and the
`delta_score` **margin** (0.08). Unlike the Stage-3 classifier settings, these
have no library default to fall back on, so they are justified empirically.

---

## The short answer

Both candidate thresholds sit **exactly on the boundary of the zero-false-positive
region**. The two control sections contain no tumor, so any refined tumor call
there is a false positive by construction. Sweeping each threshold and carrying
every setting through the full pipeline:

- `score_tumor` > 0.2 is the **most permissive floor** at which the pipeline
  returns zero tumor calls in control tissue. Loosening it to 0.15 or 0.10
  admits a false positive; tightening it to 0.25 discards 21.6% of the final
  tumor set and buys no purity, because control tissue is already at zero.
- `delta_score` > 0.08 is the **smallest margin** at which control tissue
  returns zero. At 0.06, nine false positives survive Stage 3; at 0.04, sixteen;
  with no margin at all, thirty-one. Tightening to 0.10 costs 25.4% of the final
  set for no measurable gain.

This is a stated decision rule — *maximum sensitivity subject to zero false
positives in tumor-free tissue* — not a hand-picked number, and it lands on the
values already in use.

The reference floor (0.4) is a weaker case and should be argued differently: as
an anchor-purity criterion, not an optimum. See the caveat below.

## Copy-ready: Methods

> The thresholds were calibrated empirically. SingleR provides a default
> call-quality filter (`pruneScores`, based on median-absolute-deviation outlier
> detection of the per-label score delta) but no absolute score cut-off, and its
> scores are 0.8-quantile Spearman correlations rather than calibrated
> probabilities, so no universal cut-off such as 0.5 applies: the median
> `score_tumor` among Tumor-called cells was 0.16-0.21 across the six sections,
> and a 0.5 cut-off would have retained 240 of 209,081 calls (0.12%). The two
> sham-injected control sections contain no tumor, so every cell the pipeline
> assigns to the tumor class in those sections is a false positive by
> construction; this provides a directly observable error rate against which
> thresholds can be calibrated. Each threshold was swept across a range of
> values and every setting was carried through the complete pipeline, so that
> sensitivity was assessed on the final refined tumor set rather than on the
> intermediate candidate count. The candidate `score_tumor` floor and the
> `delta_score` margin were set to the most permissive values at which the
> pipeline returned no tumor calls in either control section. The
> tumor-reference floor is not pinned by this criterion - the control sections
> return zero false positives across the entire range tested - and was instead
> set to 0.40 as a compromise between anchor contamination (0.24%) and anchor
> count (863); the dependence of the final tumor set on this choice is reported
> in Supplementary Table S1c.

## Copy-ready: Results

> Sweeping the candidate `score_tumor` floor from 0.10 to 0.30 (Supplementary
> Table S1a) showed that a floor of 0.2 was the most permissive setting at which
> both control sections yielded zero refined tumor calls; floors of 0.15 and
> 0.10 each admitted one false positive, while a floor of 0.25 removed 21.6% of
> the refined tumor set (20,873 to 16,357 cells) without improving control-tissue
> specificity, which was already perfect. The `delta_score` margin behaved
> equivalently (Supplementary Table S1b): a margin of 0.08 was the smallest
> value at which control tissue returned zero refined tumor calls, whereas
> margins of 0.06, 0.04 and 0 left 9, 16 and 31 false positives respectively,
> and a margin of 0.10 discarded a further 25.4% of the refined set. Both
> candidate thresholds therefore lie on the boundary of the zero-false-positive
> region, and were retained at that boundary.
>
> The tumor-reference floor governs the purity of the anchor set used to train
> the Stage-3 classifier (Supplementary Table S1c). Unlike the candidate
> thresholds it is not constrained by the control-tissue criterion, which
> returned zero refined tumor calls at every floor tested. Applying the anchor
> filter to the control sections, where any tumor call is spurious, gave anchor
> purities of 99.04%, 99.41% and 99.76% at floors of 0.30, 0.35 and 0.40, with
> floors of 0.45 and 0.50 eliminating the remaining contamination entirely; over
> the same range the anchor set fell from 5,165 to 140 cells and the refined
> tumor set varied from 28,730 to 6,931 cells, a factor of four. A floor of 0.40
> was adopted as a compromise between these two quantities, retaining 863 anchors
> at 99.76% purity. Out-of-fold AUROC rose monotonically with the floor (0.974 to
> 0.999), but this reflects the increasing extremity of the retained anchors
> rather than a better-specified model, and was not used as a selection
> criterion.

## Caveats to be aware of before you submit

**1. The reference floor of 0.4 is the weakest of the three.** The
zero-false-positive rule that pins down 0.2 and 0.08 cannot adjudicate it: every
floor from 0.30 to 0.50 gives zero control-tissue calls, so the control criterion
is saturated and silent here. What the floor actually trades is anchor purity
against final sensitivity, and the final tumor set varies by a factor of four
across the range tested (28,730 at 0.30 down to 6,931 at 0.50). The 0.25%
contamination tolerance is a stated choice, not an emergent optimum — present it
that way rather than implying the data selected it.

**2. Do not cite the rising AUROC as support.** It climbs from 0.974 to 0.999 as
the floor tightens purely because the anchors become more extreme and therefore
easier to separate. Quoting it as evidence that a higher floor gives a better
model would be wrong, and is the kind of thing a careful reader will catch.

**3. Verify the SingleR pruning claim before using it.** Across all six sections,
`pruned_label` is non-null for all 209,081 Tumor calls — SingleR's own quality
filter removed nothing, which is the strongest argument for needing explicit
thresholds at all. But from the CSVs alone I cannot distinguish "pruning ran and
rejected nothing" from "pruning was never enabled". Check whether `prune = TRUE`
was set in the R script. If pruning did run, add this sentence to the Methods
paragraph:

> SingleR's built-in score-pruning step removed none of the 209,081 tumor calls
> across the six sections and therefore provided no usable call-quality filter,
> motivating the explicit thresholds described below.

**4. The two candidate thresholds were swept independently, not jointly.** Each
sweep holds the other at its adopted value. A joint 2-D sweep could in principle
find a different pair on the zero-false-positive boundary — for instance a lower
floor with a larger margin. This is worth one sentence of acknowledgement if you
expect a methods-focused examiner.
